The model

Not two firms with a referral arrangement.

The usual shape is an Indian chartered accountant, a US CPA, and a founder in the middle translating between them. Each is competent. Neither is accountable for the combination — and the combination is where the group actually lives.

We are structured as one engagement covering both sides: one scope, one calendar, one set of books that reconciles before it is reported. When an Indian transfer pricing position has to agree with a US return, the same team holds both ends of it.

The firm — BNG Advisors Private Limited, operating as USAIndiaCFO — has been doing this since 2014: chartered accountants, US CPAs and legal professionals under one roof, working New York and India hours.

Six commitments
we can be held to.

Deliberately specific. A principle you cannot be caught breaking is not a principle.

01 One party owns the calendar Split compliance between two firms and the obligations that fall between them belong to nobody. We take the whole calendar or we say which parts we are not taking.
02 Scope in writing, before onboarding What is included, what is not, and what it costs — agreed before work starts rather than discovered in an invoice.
03 The people doing the work are the people you talk to No account layer between you and whoever is preparing the return.
04 We say no to work we should not take If a structure needs a specialist we are not, or the answer is "you do not need us yet", that is what you will hear.
05 Bad news early An overdue filing or a nexus you have already triggered gets raised on the call it is found, not in a quarterly review.
06 Both time zones are staffed India and US hours are covered by people who can answer, not a queue.

The failures are rarely technical. They are handoffs — a structure right in Bengaluru and wrong in Delaware, a filing nobody owned, a close that took three weeks because two ledgers disagreed.

Refusing to hand the seam to somebody else is the whole idea. Everything else — the practice areas, the monthly rhythm, the reporting — follows from it.
